# ADR-0064: A work's cast is a table with per-row provenance, and it holds the only tombstone in the schema

Source of truth: this wiki page.

- **Area:** [§3 The SQLite Contract: Durability, Locking, Migrations and Deletion](ADR-Index.md#area-3)
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Version:** unreleased

**Decided.** 0048 moves a work's cast out of `movies.cast_json` — a JSON blob a metadata fetch wrote whole — into `work_cast`, one row per (work, character, actor), pointed at a book or a `movies` row by the same polymorphic `(kind, work_id)` pair `work_reads` uses. Every row carries an `origin`: `provider` (seeded by a fetch, never touched), `corrected` (seeded, then edited), `reader` (typed by hand) or `removed` (a tombstone). The rule the column exists for is that **a refetch never overwrites a row the reader has touched**: it may add new provider rows and rewrite untouched ones, and it may not change the character or the actor on a row somebody has edited, nor resurrect one they deleted.

**Why a table.** A column cannot hold a fact two parties own. The provider owns the billed cast and rewrites it on every fetch; the reader owns the corrections and the credits nobody published. Letting the reader edit the blob instead would have worked perfectly until the first refetch, at which point every correction disappears with no trace it was ever made. And the blob was unusable for half the library anyway: it has never had an edit surface anywhere in the app, and it is empty for nearly every game, because a game's voice credits come from Wikidata joined on the IGDB slug and TIP-META-018 measures that coverage at 10 usable casts out of 24 titles.

**This is a departure from §3's "hard delete everywhere — no tombstones", and it is the only one.** Deleting a provider's cast row sets `origin = 'removed'` and keeps the row, because the alternative is a refetch that silently undoes the deletion for ever — the provider will list that person again, and something has to tell the merge not to take it. The exception is bounded three ways: it applies only to rows a provider seeded (a reader-authored row is hard-deleted, because nothing will ever re-add it), the tombstone is invisible to every read but the merge, and it is reaped with its work by the table's two AFTER DELETE triggers. The pair `UNIQUE` is partial — `WHERE origin <> 'removed'` — precisely so a tombstone never refuses the reader their own row back; typing the pair in again revives the tombstone in place.

**What the merge rule costs, stated rather than discovered.** A name the reader "fixed" wrongly stays wrong for ever, even after the provider later agrees with the truth. The only way back is to delete the row and let the next fetch re-seed it. The alternative cost — a refetch quietly discarding an edit somebody made on purpose — is the failure this feature exists to refuse, and between a wrong name you can see and an edit that vanished without a trace, the first is the one its author can fix.

**`movies.cast_json` is not dropped and is still written**, following 0036 and 0037 exactly: 0036 kept `utterances.category` "deliberately and for one release" because *"dropping a column is the one migration step that cannot be walked back by hand"*, and 0037's backfill is what it was kept for. One strengthening on that case: `category` was a value the reader typed and could retype, while a cast comes from a provider that may be unreachable or unkeyed next week — so if 0048's backfill is wrong about a library, the frozen blob is the only copy in existence. `dialogues.actor` is not touched either; the mapping becomes the source that fills it, not a replacement for it, and it stays stored, FTS-indexed, faceted, exported and imported.

**With one exception, and it is the difference between "kept" and "frozen".** The two paths that replace a title's whole record — the create-from-source insert and `resyncMovieFromSource` — go on writing the blob whole. `applyReverifyMovie` writes it **only where it is empty**, `CASE WHEN COALESCE(cast_json,'') IN ('','[]')`, because that function is also what a fill applies through — the `fill` job Fill gaps starts, and `POST /metadata/fill` for an API caller — and a fill is unattended and bulk: up to two thousand titles a job, no diff on screen. A resync is one title somebody asked for by name; a fill is a button that can walk a library, and a column kept solely so a mistake is repairable cannot be spent by the path with no human in it. Filling an empty blob rather than refusing outright was originally to keep the column's **last remaining reader** working on a title that never had one — the quiz's speaker distractors. **That reader has since moved to `work_cast`, and the freeze is why**: an approved cast diff writes the mapping and no longer writes the blob, so the pool went stale for good while `resyncMovieFromSource` went on replacing the blob whole, and one title answered *"who else is in this film?"* two different ways depending on which button was pressed. Nothing reads the column now. The conditional write stays for a smaller reason that is still true: the `CASE` is what keeps `cast_json` in the UPDATE's column list, so a cast-only approval has a statement to prove ownership with rather than falling through to "no approved fields".

**THE ACTOR→PORTRAIT RESOLVER MOVED TO THE MAPPING, and it had to.** `actorPortraitFromCast` pins an actor to a supplier's person id taken from the cast of a film they are in — the film is the disambiguator, so the id is exact — and its fallback is a by-name person search its own comment calls namesake-prone (§ *Pin a person, not a name*). Reading the blob meant the two things this table adds were the two the resolver could not see: **a name the reader corrects** (the mapping fills `dialogues.actor`, so the chip shows the corrected spelling while the blob still held the provider's, and the lookup missed) and **a game's entire voice cast** (for most games the blob is `'[]'`). It now reads `work_cast` by `actor_key` — the row carries `person_id` and `image_url` itself — with tombstones excluded, and a row's own `source` word instead of one guessed from the film's `tmdb_id`/`tvdb_id`. That last change makes `wikidata` reachable, so `resolveActorMeta` keeps a Wikidata QID exactly as it already kept a TVDB id: it is not a TMDB person id, it cannot be handed to `/person/{id}`, and a bio is not worth swapping a correct identity for a namesake's. The dialogue join is gone with the blob — a cast row no longer needs a quote beside it to be found, which was an artefact of the blob being reachable only through the film.

**THE UNATTENDED FILL ASKS THE TABLE, NOT THE VALUE.** `missingStored` is dispatched on TYPE so a field added to re-verify tomorrow needs no edit there, and a cast is the one field whose emptiness is not in its value: deleting every credit leaves an empty list and a tombstone per row, and a tombstone is filtered out of every read but the merge's. So the diff's stored side is honestly empty, `missingStored` honestly says "missing", and fill offered back the list somebody had just finished deleting — reporting `filled: ["cast"]` over a merge that correctly wrote nothing, which is the worse half. `fillOne` therefore asks `castCurated` by field name, once, with the reason written down.

**Two things the rule deliberately does not cover, both worth stating because both look like breaches of it.** First, `billing`, `person_id`, `image_url` and `source` are the provider's own facts, and **a refetch takes them back on every row regardless of who has touched it** — no edit surface will ever offer them, and they are what the portrait pipeline and the quiz's distractor ordering read. What the rule protects is the character and the actor, and nothing else. Second, **an empty fetched list is not a retraction**: `mergeProviderCast` returns without doing anything when the provider lists nobody, where the blob wrote `'[]'` and erased the lot. This app has a cast lookup that comes back empty as a matter of course — a game's voice credits are a second, best-effort Wikidata request allowed to fail so that a failure there cannot fail the whole fetch — so "the provider listed nobody" and "nothing asked the provider" are the same bytes, and a title genuinely losing its entire cast does not happen. One row being retracted still drops that row.

**ADOPTION, the case the rule's four states do not name.** The reader types a credit the provider has not published yet, and the provider catches up. The merge looks a fetched entry up by the folded pair before inserting it, and claims the existing row — writing the provider key onto it and leaving its `origin` alone. Without that step the insert would hit the pair `UNIQUE` and turn "look this up again" into a 500 because somebody had already got a name right by hand. The same lookup is what finds a row when a *provider* changes its own capitalisation, which changes `provider_key` while the folded pair stays put — including on a tombstone, so a casing change cannot resurrect a deleted row by the side door.

**`provider_key` IS FROZEN AT SEED, and adoption is where that is enforced: it writes a key onto a row that has none and never rewrites one that has.** Re-keying the row to match the provider's new casing is the obvious move and it is wrong, because **one row can be reached by two fetched entries** — once by key, once by folded pair. A provider billing both `("Neo","Keanu Reeves")` and `("neo","keanu reeves")`, which is the double billing the migration's backfill has an `INSERT OR IGNORE` for, then leaves the row wearing whichever key arrived second, and the next fetch swaps it back: the key flips on every refetch, and it is the anchor the retraction pass and `carryWorkCast` both read a row's provenance through. The same double billing is also why a row this loop **inserts** is marked as seen — unmarked, the second entry came back through adoption and overwrote the first's names and billing. First entry wins, which is the answer the backfill already gives the same data. The freeze costs one extra indexed lookup per re-cased entry per fetch, and one honest consequence: a provider row whose supplier has re-cased a name carries a key that no longer equals `ProviderKey` of its own names. Nothing reads it that way, and `applyImportedCast`, which rebuilds a key from the names, records what that costs there.

**A DEPARTURE, recorded as one: `refillMovieActors` fills only an EMPTY `dialogues.actor` and never rewrites a name already there.** So correcting a cast row does not flow through to lines that already carry the old actor. That is deliberate — an actor the reader typed must not be silently rewritten by a metadata edit — and the deliberate remedy already exists and has to be asked for: `POST /movies/{id}/remap-speakers`. Which now reads the mapping too, so the remap and the refill inside one request can no longer disagree about who plays somebody; they used to read the blob and the blob respectively, then the blob and the table.

**`movieActors` stays derived from LINES and not from the cast**, unchanged, for the reason `movie_actors_test.go` argues: the catalogue's actor filter means "whose lines do I have", not "who is in the cast". It is the one site this table must not be plumbed into.

**Instead of.** A `cast` field on the movie edit form writing the same blob (rejected: the first refetch erases every correction, invisibly). A composite primary key on `(kind, work_id, character_key)` (rejected: correcting a spelling is the feature, and under a composite key that is a primary-key update — the provenance flag would hang off an identity the edit itself changes). Seeding a book's list from `DISTINCT annotations.character` (rejected: that files every misspelling as its own character). Keying the merge on the character alone (rejected: a provider legitimately bills one character twice — young and old Vito, a recast part — so the key is the pair, with `ORDER BY billing LIMIT 1` reproducing the blob loop's first-match-wins for the auto-fill).

**A MERGE CARRIES THE CAST, AND THAT IS THE RULE APPLIED TO A SECOND SOURCE OF TRUTH.** `POST /movies/merge` and `POST /books/merge` re-point the quotes and then hard-delete the source rows, so the table's AFTER DELETE triggers reaped their cast — every voice actor the reader had typed on the duplicate, every correction, every tombstone, with no bin snapshot to recover from because a merge takes none. `carryWorkCast` moves them onto the survivor before the delete. Where both works name the same folded pair, **the survivor's row stays** — the pair `UNIQUE` allows exactly one, and the survivor is what the reader is looking at — but its `origin` is lifted from `provider` to `corrected` if the row it absorbed was the reader's, because otherwise the next refetch would rewrite a name they had already fixed on the copy they merged away. **A live row beats a tombstone, whichever side holds it**, and a dropped tombstone gets a trace line naming the pair rather than vanishing: a merge is additive everywhere else, so removing a credit the reader can *see* on the survivor because they had deleted it from a duplicate is not a trade anybody asked for. It is the one statement in `handleMergeMovies` that is shared with `handleMergeBooks` rather than mirrored, because `work_cast` is one table addressed by `(kind, work_id)` and there is no per-side table name or person kind in it to get subtly wrong.

**THE CAST ROUND-TRIPS THROUGH THE MARKDOWN EXPORT, `origin` AND TOMBSTONES INCLUDED.** `work_reads` is cited above as the precedent this table copied, and the export is the one place `work_reads` actually round-trips — so the half that had not been copied was the one that matters most for a game. One frontmatter line, `cast:` on a title and `characters:` on a book, shaped after `reads:` down to the separators and read back by the same kind of hand-rolled split: `cast: GLaDOS — Ellen McLain; Chell (reader); Cave Johnson — J.K. Simmons (removed)`. `provider` is written as no marker at all, so a film nobody has touched exports as the plain list a person would write and a work with no cast exports byte-identically to before. The provenance is on the wire because it is the half that cannot be re-derived: without it an export hands the library back with every correction demoted to the provider's and every deletion undone by the next lookup, and a character the reader typed and had not yet quoted was lost outright — the per-line `actor` binding was the only trace the file carried. `person_id` and `image_url` are deliberately left out (an id in a supplier's namespace and a URL do not belong in a file a person edits, and the next lookup takes them back anyway), which costs a portrait per row until then. On the way in it is **fill-empty-only and whole-list-or-nothing**, exactly as the read log is: a work that already has one cast row ignores the file's list entirely, so a two-year-old export cannot undo last week's correction. A book's entries are **cleared of their actors rather than refused**, which is 0047's rule — the API rejects a field the kind does not have, an import clears it — and it is what makes retargeting a film's file onto a book work. `staged_works.cast_json` exists because staging sits in the middle of that round trip; 0034 records the same near-miss for `translator`, on the same wrong reasoning that no third-party importer has a source for one.

**WHAT ENFORCES THE RULE IS THE `origin` WORD, IN TWO PLACES, AND TWO DOCUMENTED MECHANISMS TURNED OUT NOT TO BE AMONG THEM.** A review pass deleted each of the protections this entry and 0048's header named, and the suite went on passing — which is worse than an unprotected row, because a comment claiming an invariant with nothing pinning it is how the next person breaks it silently. First, a reader-authored row was said to be *"protected by construction, not by a check"*, on the grounds that it has no `provider_key` and the merge reads only rows that have one. That holds until the provider catches up with the credit: `adoptCastRow` then gives the row a key **on purpose**, so the listing is re-matched rather than duplicated beside it, and from that fetch on the row is in the merge's set like any other. The `provider_key <> ''` predicate is a narrowing — it keeps rows out of a map keyed by a column they do not have — and never a protection. Second, the merge's empty `case removed:` was said to be *"the whole of its enforcement"* of the no-resurrection rule, when **nothing in the merge writes `origin` at all**: a tombstone leaves every branch of that switch as a tombstone, and what makes it invisible is `handleDeleteCast` keeping the row plus the `origin <> 'removed'` filter on every read outside the merge. So the live mechanisms are the switch's `default:` (the provider's facts, never a name) and the retraction pass's `origin = 'provider'` test — and each now fails a named test in `cast_protection_test.go` when it is removed, reached by the one thing no earlier test did: a provider that **stops listing** an entry, or **re-cases its own names**.

**The empty `removed` case is kept, with a narrower claim that is true.** A tombstone records what the reader deleted **in the words they were looking at**. It is the one row in the table nobody can see, so the Markdown export is the only place it is ever shown back to them, and a supplier re-casing a name reaches it through `adoptCastRow`'s folded-pair lookup — where `updateProviderCastRow` would respell their deletion and re-bill it to the head of the file's `cast:` line. Letting it gains nothing, since `billing`, `person_id` and `image_url` on a row that is not on the list are read by nobody. That lookup **returning the tombstone rather than passing over it** was itself unpinned and is the same test's first assertion: exclude tombstones there and a re-cased refetch inserts a fresh live row beside the tombstone, resurrecting the credit by the side door.

**THE QUIZ'S SPEAKER DISTRACTORS READ THE MAPPING, AND THE DEFERRAL THAT SAID THEY WOULD NOT DID NOT SURVIVE THE FREEZE ABOVE.** The plan was to leave `castActors` on `movies.cast_json` until the commit that **drops the column**, recorded as `TestAGamesTypedVoiceCastIsNotYetAQuizDistractor` — a test that asserted the defect and was written to fail the day somebody wired the mapping in, so a known gap was announced rather than discovered. Making `applyReverifyMovie`'s write fill-only is what forced it: a frozen column with a reader on it is a pool that **goes stale the first time a cast diff is approved and never recovers**, while `resyncMovieFromSource` still rewrites the blob whole. Unfreezing the blob hands back the defect the freeze fixed (the unattended bulk fill spending the only pre-0048 copy there is), so the reader moved instead — `quizPools` now reads `work_cast`, excluding tombstones (a credit the reader deleted is not an option) and rows with no actor (a book's characters, and a game credit whose voice actor is unknown; a nameless distractor is not a distractor). It lands three things at once: the pool stops going stale, **a game's typed voice cast is finally a distractor** — the blob is `'[]'` for nearly every game, so there was never anything there to offer — and `cast_json` loses its last reader, which is the precondition for dropping it. The tripwire test is inverted rather than deleted — `TestAGamesTypedVoiceCastFeedsTheQuiz` asserts the credits it used to assert the absence of.

**Approved.** Mine, on the owner's brief, which stated the merge rule as non-negotiable.
